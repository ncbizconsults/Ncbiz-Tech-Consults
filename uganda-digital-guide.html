Where Are Your Customers Online in Uganda?

The answer isn't just "whichever platform has the biggest number." It depends on your business, who you're trying to reach, and what you want the internet to do for you. Here's the real data — and how to read it.

Uganda's Digital Picture

The big numbers, at a glance.

40.9M
Active mobile connections in Uganda — equivalent to about 79% of the population (some people hold more than one SIM).
DataReportal, Digital 2026 Uganda — late 2025
11.4M
People actually using the internet in Uganda — about 22% of the population.
DataReportal, Digital 2026 Uganda — late 2025
2.4M
Active social media user identities in Uganda — about 4.6% of the population.
DataReportal / Kepios — October 2025

These three numbers already tell a story: most Ugandans with a phone connection are not yet "internet users" in the full sense, and a smaller group again are active on social media specifically. Meanwhile, individual platforms like TikTok and WhatsApp report far higher subscriber counts than that 2.4M figure — because they're measuring something different. We explain exactly why further down this page.

What Each Channel Is Good For

Not every business needs every platform.

TikTok

Discovery and attention through short-form video. Strong for businesses with something visual or entertaining to show.

Instagram

Visual presentation — fashion, beauty, food, products, finished work. Good for building a brand look.

Facebook

Reach, community and broad local audience engagement, where accessible.

WhatsApp

Direct conversations — enquiries, quotes, order updates, and closing a sale one-on-one.

Google Search / Maps

People actively looking for a business, product or service right now — often the highest-intent channel.

A Website

Deeper information, credibility and conversion — the place people go once they're already interested.

YouTube

Education, demonstrations and longer-form video — explaining something in depth.

LinkedIn

Professional and B2B audiences — useful mainly if you sell to other businesses or organizations.

The Bigger Picture

Social media is not the whole internet.

Google, your Google Business Profile, your website, WhatsApp and social media all play different roles. Most businesses don't need to choose one — they need each piece doing the job it's actually good at, working together rather than competing.

The Customer Journey

How people move from finding you to contacting you.

1

Discover

Someone sees your TikTok, Instagram post, Google search result, or hears about you by word of mouth.

2

Learn & Trust

They check your website or Google Business Profile to see if you're legitimate, what you offer, and whether you're worth their time.

3

Contact

They message you on WhatsApp, call, or fill in a form — this is where interest becomes a conversation.

4

Buy / Enquire

The conversation turns into a sale, a booking, or a confirmed enquiry.

Reading the Data Correctly

What the numbers really mean.

Why does UCC say 35.7M social media subscriptions, while DataReportal says only 2.4M active identities?

They're measuring completely different things, and both are correct within their own methodology. UCC's figure is the sum of subscription counts reported per platform — this can include inactive accounts, multiple accounts per person, and one person subscribed to several platforms at once, each counted separately. DataReportal's 2.4M figure (research by Kepios) is an estimate of genuinely active, de-duplicated social media identities. Neither number is "the real one" — they answer different questions. Subscriptions tell you platform-level reach; active identities tell you roughly how many real people are genuinely active.

A similar caution applies to mobile connections (40.9M) versus internet users (11.4M) versus social media identities (2.4M) — each step down measures a narrower, more specific behaviour. And website traffic figures (like Statcounter's page-view shares) measure visits, not people — one person can generate many page views, and the same person can appear in multiple platforms' numbers. Use these figures to understand direction and scale, not as precise headcounts.
