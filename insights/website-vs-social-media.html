Website vs Facebook, Instagram & WhatsApp: which is better for a business?

It's not really a competition — each channel does something the others can't.

This gets asked a lot, usually framed as a choice: website or Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp? In practice it isn't a competition — each of these does something different, and the businesses that grow fastest online tend to use them together rather than picking one.

What each channel is actually good at

Facebook and Instagram are discovery and attention — where people encounter your business for the first time, see your work, and get a feel for your brand through posts and engagement. They're free to start, fast to update, and reach people scrolling who weren't specifically looking for you. WhatsApp is where the actual conversation happens — questions get answered, orders get confirmed, trust gets built one-on-one. In Uganda specifically, WhatsApp Business is genuinely core infrastructure for a lot of small businesses already, not a nice-to-have — many sellers run their entire order and delivery process through a WhatsApp catalog and direct messages. Google search is a different kind of discovery — people who don't yet know your business but are actively looking for what you offer. A website is what ties it together: the owned, structured place that explains what you do properly, builds credibility, and is what Google can actually show to a stranger searching for your services.

Where social media alone falls short

A social media page is genuinely useful, but it's borrowed space — the platform decides what changes, what your reach looks like, and what features you get. It's also hard for a new visitor to quickly understand what you offer from a feed of posts, compared to a website's clear structure. And social platforms generally don't get shown to someone Googling your type of business the way a website can.

The combination that actually works

Google, your website, your social pages and WhatsApp aren't competing channels — they're stages of the same journey. Someone finds you through a Google search, an Instagram post, or a friend's WhatsApp forward. They check your website to understand what you do and decide if they trust you. They message you on WhatsApp to ask questions or place an order. Each channel does the part it's good at; trying to make one channel do all of it usually means all of it happens worse. This connects directly to our Digital Marketing service, which is built around exactly this — using each channel for what it's actually good at rather than treating them as substitutes for each other.
