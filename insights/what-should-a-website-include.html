What should a business website include?

The right building blocks, and why the same template rarely fits every business.

There's no single template that fits every business — a consulting firm, a boutique, and a farm-produce exporter need customers to see and do different things. But most genuinely useful business websites share a handful of building blocks, adapted to what the business actually needs.

The core elements most businesses need

A clear homepage that says what you do and who it's for within a few seconds — not just a slogan. Services or products, described in terms a customer understands, not internal jargon. An About section that builds trust — who you are, why you're credible. Contact information that's actually easy to find — phone, WhatsApp, email, location — not buried at the bottom of one page. Clear calls to action telling the visitor what to do next: message on WhatsApp, request a quote, call, visit. Trust signals — real work you've done, genuine reviews, relevant credentials — without inventing anything you don't actually have.

What's often missed

Mobile usability — most visitors in Uganda will land on your site from a phone, so it needs to actually work well there, not just "not be broken." Basic SEO foundations — sensible page titles, descriptions and structure, so Google can understand and show your site, covered in depth in how to get your website on Google. A FAQs section, which answers the questions you're tired of repeating in DMs. Analytics, so you actually know whether the site is doing anything — even a simple visitor count is better than guessing.

Structure depends on the business

A service business usually needs individual pages per service, since each one has a different customer and a different pitch. A product business needs clear categories and pricing. A business that's mainly about credibility (consulting, professional services) needs a strong About and case studies/portfolio more than a large page count. This is exactly why website projects should start with a conversation about your specific business rather than a fixed template — a 4-page Business Starter Website structured well for your actual customers beats a generic 10-page template that doesn't match how people actually decide to contact you.

When you'll want more structure

As a business grows — more services, more content to publish, more people involved in managing the site — the structure needs to grow with it. That's the difference between a Professional Business Website with CMS, built for regular content updates, and a Premium Business Growth Website with Advanced CMS, built for larger content structures and multiple people managing it. The right structure is the one that matches where your business actually is — not the biggest one available. More on how we approach this on the main Website Development page.
