How do I get my business website on Google?

Building a website and getting it visible on Google are related — but they are not the same thing.

A common surprise after launching a new website: it doesn't show up on Google, even days or weeks later. This is normal, not a sign something's broken — building a website and getting it visible on Google are related but genuinely separate things, and the second one takes deliberate work.

Why a new website doesn't rank automatically

Google has to first discover your website exists, then crawl it, then decide it's worth showing for relevant searches — none of which happens the moment you publish. A brand-new site with no history, thin content, or no signals pointing to it has nothing yet to tell Google it deserves to rank. This is true no matter how good the design is — visibility and design are different problems.

The practical fundamentals

Submit your site to Google Search Console and provide a sitemap — this is how you actively tell Google your pages exist, rather than waiting to be discovered. Write titles and meta descriptions for every page that actually describe what's on it and match what someone would search for. Structure content around real search intent — the actual questions and phrases a potential customer would type, not just your own internal language for your services. Use internal links connecting related pages on your own site, which helps both visitors and Google understand how your content fits together. Make sure it works properly on mobile — Google evaluates the mobile version of your site first. Get the technical basics right — pages that load reasonably fast, no broken links, no accidental settings blocking Google from indexing pages.

Don't skip your Google Business Profile

For a Uganda-based business serving local customers, a properly completed Google Business Profile — accurate hours, address, phone, category, photos, and genuine reviews — often matters as much as the website itself for local visibility. It's frequently what shows up first when someone searches for your type of business nearby, and it links straight back to your website.

This takes ongoing attention, not a one-time setup

SEO isn't a switch you flip once at launch — it's an ongoing part of how the site is maintained: adding genuinely useful content over time, keeping information accurate, fixing issues as they come up. It works best when it's built into the site from the start rather than added afterward as an afterthought, which is how we approach every project on our Website Development page.
