Do I really need a website for my small business?

Short answer: usually yes, but not always right away — and not always the biggest version of one. Here's how to think about it properly.

If your business already has an active Facebook or Instagram page and a WhatsApp number customers message you on, it's a fair question: why spend money on a website too? For a genuinely early-stage business — testing an idea, no fixed customer base yet, minimal budget — social media and WhatsApp alone can carry you for a while. That's a reasonable place to start, not a failure.

Where social media and WhatsApp fall short

The gap shows up as the business grows. A social media page is something you're renting space on, not something you own — the platform can change its algorithm, restrict your account, or simply become less useful to you overnight, and none of that is in your control. When someone searches Google for what you offer rather than scrolling their feed, a social page rarely shows up the way a real website can. And a customer who wants to quickly check what you actually do, what you charge, and whether you're legitimate often has to scroll through months of posts to find that — a website puts it in one place, immediately.

What a website actually adds

Credibility is the first thing. A proper website signals that a business is established, not improvised — that matters more in some sectors (professional services, larger purchases) than others. Second is Google visibility: a website gives you something that can actually rank and be found by people who don't already know you exist. Third is control — your information, structured the way you want it, not subject to a platform's layout decisions or feed algorithm. Fourth, practically, is that a website works as a place to send people: a link on your WhatsApp Business profile, in a Facebook bio, on a signboard, or in a Google Business Profile — all pointing somewhere you fully control.

Website and social media aren't competing

This isn't really a case of choosing one over the other. Social media and WhatsApp are usually where people first discover you and where the actual back-and-forth conversation happens — that's what they're good at. A website is where that person goes to properly understand what you offer and decide to trust you, and where Google sends people who weren't already following you anywhere. Used together, social media brings attention, WhatsApp handles the conversation, and the website does the converting and the credibility-building.

So when do you actually need one?

A website earns its cost once one or more of these is true: customers regularly ask "do you have a website?" or search for you on Google and can't find much; you're spending money on a signboard, business cards or ads that need somewhere consistent to point to; you're tired of re-explaining pricing and services in every single DM; or you're ready to be found by people who've never heard of you, not just people already following your page. If none of that applies yet, it's genuinely fine to wait — a rushed, half-finished website often does less for a business than a well-run WhatsApp Business profile and social page.

If you decide you're ready

You don't need to start with the biggest possible option. A straightforward Business Starter Website is usually the right first step — a clear, professional presence covering who you are, what you do, and how to reach you. If you already know you'll be updating prices, services or blog content regularly yourself, it's worth looking at a Professional Business Website with CMS instead, so you're not waiting on a developer for routine changes. Either way, the starting point is the same: understanding what the website actually needs to do for your business, covered in more detail on our Website Development page.
